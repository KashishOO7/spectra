## stories
### lock-your-accounts
A site you signed up to years ago is broken into, and your password is taken.
A program tries that password on your email and on other sites.
With two-factor authentication on, the password alone does not get it in.
### spot-a-scam
A message says your account will be closed today unless you act now.
Its link and its phone number both lead back to whoever sent it.
You close it and contact the company on a number you already had.
### ai-and-fakes
A caller sounds exactly like someone in your family and asks for money now.
A voice can be copied from a short clip, and the number on your screen can be faked.
You ask for your family's secret word, and the caller cannot give it.
### protect-your-phone
Your phone is left behind on a train.
Whoever picks it up finds a lock screen that asks for your code.
With encryption on, what is stored on the phone cannot be read without that code.
### who-can-find-you
Someone searches for your name and your town.
A people-search site shows your address, phone number and relatives' names.
You ask the site to remove that page, and search again later in case it comes back.
### cut-down-tracking
You open a website to read one article.
Its trackers can tell dozens of other companies that you were there.
With those trackers blocked in your browser, fewer companies learn about your visit.
### if-something-happens
You realise the money you just sent went to a scammer.
You tell your bank, or the app you paid through, straight away.
The sooner they know, the better the chance of getting the money back.
### looking-after-someone
Someone your child met in a game is friendly and gives them free things.
Then they ask to move to a private app and to keep it a secret.
Your child tells you, because you talked about these warning signs together before.
