## description
Spectra in about a minute: plain steps in order, one at a time, saved in your browser. Pick the risks that matter most to you and their steps come first.

## heading
How Spectra works

## lead
Spectra is a free security playbook: guided steps, shown one at a time, with the steps for your situation first.

## reading-time
About a minute to read.

## steps
### You answer five questions, or skip them
Answer five yes-or-no questions, or on the [You](/you) page pick the risks that matter most to you, such as "Someone gets into your accounts". You can also skip both and go straight to the steps, and change your answers whenever you like.

### You get the steps in order
Every step that applies to everyone is in your playbook, and the ones that fit what you answered come first. You see one at a time rather than a wall of everything at once.

### You do one thing
Each step is a single action in plain words, with the setting named for your phone or your computer. If a step does not apply to you, click or tap "Doesn't apply to me" and it stops coming up as your next step.

### It remembers where you got to
What you have done is saved in this browser, so you can stop, close the tab, and pick it up later from the same place.

## why-heading
Why the order changes

## why
Your answers change the order of your playbook, and some situations add steps written for them. Somebody worried about a person they know needs different steps first from somebody worried about scams sent to millions of people at once, and gets some steps that only apply to them.

Every step says why it is there, and the [Method and sources](/methodology) page shows how the order is calculated, including what the ordering cannot do.

## limits-heading
What Spectra does not do

## limits
### It never looks at your accounts, files or devices
Spectra has no way to see any of them. Nothing it shows you is based on checking anything of yours.

### What you choose never leaves this browser
There is no account and nothing to sign up for, and nothing you choose is sent to a server. The page loads its fonts from Google's servers, and nothing you choose goes with that request.

### Spectra does not tell you what will happen to you
Each step says what is generally worth doing and links the source it came from, so you can read the reasoning and decide for yourself.
