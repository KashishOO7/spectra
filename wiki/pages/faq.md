## about
### What is Spectra?
Spectra is a free security playbook: guided steps for your accounts, money, devices and privacy, so you know what to do first, before something goes wrong and right after it does.
Each step shows how to do it on your phone or computer and lists the pages its facts come from. If something has already gone wrong, start with [Something happened](/incident).

### Who is behind Spectra?
Spectra is the personal project of Kashish, who has a master's degree in digital forensics and information security.
He built it after friends and relatives kept coming to him with the same problems: they had clicked a phishing link, had an account taken over, or were being stalked. He could not help everyone himself, so Spectra teaches the everyday habits that look after your accounts, devices and personal details. [The full story is on About](/about).

### Why should I use Spectra if nothing has gone wrong?
The steps are worth doing before you need them: doing them afterwards is harder, and recovery is not always possible. Once an account is taken over or money is sent to a scammer, getting it back depends on the service or the bank. Many of the steps are settings you change once.
Two-factor authentication is the clearest example: with it on, a password stolen from one website is not enough to get into your account. In a Microsoft study of work accounts whose passwords had leaked, accounts with two-factor authentication were 98.6% less likely to be taken over than accounts without it.
Some steps also make everyday life simpler. A password manager means one password to remember instead of many, and a private account means fewer strangers can see your posts. Spectra puts the step that matters most for your situation first, so you start with one thing instead of a long list, and you can stop after any step.

### How is Spectra different from a security checklist?
A checklist gives everyone the same list, in the same order. Spectra shows one step at a time and puts the steps for your situation first.
Every step explains how to do it and where its facts come from. Spectra also has steps for when something has already happened, and it never tells you to buy a named product.

### What does Spectra cover?
Spectra's steps cover your accounts and passwords, your money, scams, your phone and computer, the smart devices in your home and your car, where you are, what is public about you, and what to do when something has already happened.
The steps are grouped in nine chapters: [Lock your accounts](/chapter/lock-your-accounts), [Spot a scam](/chapter/spot-a-scam), [AI and fakes](/chapter/ai-and-fakes), [Protect your phone](/chapter/protect-your-phone), [Control who can find you](/chapter/who-can-find-you), [Cut down tracking](/chapter/cut-down-tracking), [If something happens](/chapter/if-something-happens), [Looking after someone](/chapter/looking-after-someone) and [Smart home](/chapter/smart-home).

### Is Spectra really free?
Yes. Spectra has no paid version and no adverts, and nobody pays to appear in it.
Spectra never tells you to buy or install a named product.

### Do I need an account?
No. There is nothing to sign up for and no sign-in. You open the site and start.

## data
### What happens to what I choose or type in Spectra?
What you choose or type stays in your browser on your device, and it is never sent to a server.
Spectra has no accounts and no database on a server. Your progress is saved in your browser's storage (IndexedDB). [The code is public](https://github.com/KashishOO7/spectra), so anyone can read it.

### Can Spectra see my accounts, phone or passwords?
No. Spectra has no way to see your accounts, files, passwords or devices, and nothing it shows you comes from checking anything of yours.
What you choose in Your setup changes which steps come first, and you mark steps done yourself.

### Does Spectra track me or use cookies?
No. Spectra has no analytics, no tracking pixels and no session recording, and the site sets no cookies.
Your browser's storage holds only what you ticked, the choices you made in Your setup, your answers in the game and the quiz, the entries in Your journey, the light or dark theme you chose, and a copy of the pages so they open offline. [More on privacy](/about#privacy).

### How do I delete everything Spectra saved?
Open [You](/you) and choose Start over, or clear this site's data in your browser's settings.
Either one removes everything Spectra kept in this browser. Single entries in [Your journey](/timeline) can also be deleted one at a time.

### Can someone else who uses my phone or computer see what I did in Spectra?
Yes. Anyone who can open the same browser on the same device can see your progress and the choices you made, and your browser's history shows that you visited the site.
If you share the device, choose Start over in [You](/you) when you finish, or use a device the other person cannot open. If you think someone is watching your phone or computer, read the guide [Someone may be watching me through my phone or computer](/incident), on a different device that the person has never had access to.

### What happens if I use a private or incognito window?
In a private or incognito window, what you do in Spectra is usually deleted when you close the window.
Chrome, for example, removes a site's data once every Incognito window is closed. To keep your progress between visits, use a normal window, or save a copy of your setup in [You](/you) before you close it.

## how
### How does Spectra decide which step comes first?
Spectra starts with the steps that prevent the most harm for most people, then moves up the steps that match what you picked.
What worries you, who might try something, or that you look after someone all change the order, and a step moves down when something you already do covers it. Nothing is ordered by hand. [Method and sources](/methodology) shows the full formula.

### What are the five questions for, and can I skip them?
The [five questions](/start) find the few things most worth doing for you now, and the first one opens straight away.
You can skip them. You get the same steps either way, and your answers only change the order.

### Do I have to do every step?
No. You can skip any step and come back to it later.
Each step says what is generally worth doing, and you decide what fits your life.

### Where does the advice come from, and can I check it?
Every step lists the pages its facts come from, so you can read them yourself.
Where no source holds up, Spectra tells you what to check for yourself instead. [Method and sources](/methodology) explains how the steps are written and checked.

### Why doesn't Spectra tell me which app to use?
A recommendation is a claim about a company, and a company can be sold, change its terms or start doing something new without Spectra knowing.
So each step explains what to look for in a tool, and [the guides](/resources) link to lists that other people maintain and update more often than Spectra could.

### Can I use Spectra for my parent or my child?
Yes. You can [set Spectra up for someone else](/audit?mode=guardian), such as a parent, a friend or a child with a first phone, and the steps for looking after someone are added.
You can also [print a list](/playbook) for someone who would rather work from paper.

### Can I move my progress to another phone or computer?
Yes, in two ways, and neither sends anything to a server.
In [You](/you) you can save your setup as a file and load it on the other device, or share it as a link or QR code that carries the setup inside the link itself. Anyone who opens that file or link can see your setup, so keep it to yourself.

### Does Spectra work without internet?
Yes, once you have opened it. Your browser keeps a copy of the pages, so they open without a connection.
Links to sources and guides on other websites still need one.

### Can I print my list?
Yes. [Choose what goes on the sheet](/playbook), then print it or save it as a PDF.

### Is there an app?
No, and you do not need one. Spectra runs in the browser on any phone, tablet or computer.

## happened
### My account was hacked. What do I do first?
Use another phone or computer, not the one you think was broken into, and open the site's Forgot password link to set a new password you have not used before.
Then sign out of every other device in the account's settings, and give any other site where you used the same password its own new password. [See all the steps](/incident).

### I clicked a link I should not have. What now?
If you typed a password on that page, change it now on the real site or app, opened yourself rather than through the link, from another device if you can. Do not wait to see whether anything happens.
Whoever made the page may try the password straight away. [See what else to do](/incident).

### I paid a scammer. What do I do?
Stop replying, and send no more money. Then tell the bank, card company, payment app or other service you paid through that it was a scam, and ask them to reverse the payment as soon as you can.
Asking is worth it even if the money seems gone. [Read the full step](/checklist/money-scam-first-hour-001).

### I am locked out of my own account. What do I do?
Start from the service's own sign-in page and its forgot password link, and have your backup codes ready if you saved any.
The service's own support staff cannot skip its checks for you, and nobody outside the service can. [Read the full step](/checklist/recovery-locked-out-001).

### Someone is pretending to be me online. What do I do?
Take screenshots of the fake account before you report it, then report it through the app or website it is on.
A removed account takes its posts with it, so the screenshots are your record of what was posted. [Read the full step](/checklist/impersonation-report-001).

### Someone will not leave me alone online. What do I do?
Save the messages and the posts now, before any of them are deleted: take a screenshot of each one, and save the link to the post or profile as well.
A screenshot with no link behind it is weaker evidence later, and a service, your workplace and the police all need this record. [Read the full step](/checklist/harassment-document-001).

### I think someone is tracking my phone. What do I do?
If you think software on your phone or computer is watching you, read this on a different device that the person has never touched.
Do not confront them and do not delete anything yet, because removing it tells them you know, and that is when these situations often get worse. [The guide](/incident) says what to do first and who can help.

### My phone or laptop was lost or stolen. What do I do?
From another device, mark it as lost as soon as you can: in Find My or at icloud.com/find for an Apple device, in the Find Hub app for Android, or at account.microsoft.com/devices for Windows, where the option is called Lock. If it is offline, it locks the next time it goes online.
Only erase it if you do not expect to get it back. [See the steps for iPhone, Android and laptops](/incident).

### My details were in a data breach. What do I do?
Change your password on the service that was breached now, even if its notice says passwords were not affected, and give any other site where you used the same password its own new password.
Open the breach notice on the service's own website, not through a link in an email. [See all the steps](/incident).

### Can Spectra get my account or money back?
No. Spectra cannot act for you or contact anyone on your behalf.
Spectra tells you what to do and who to ask, such as your bank or the service's own recovery page.

### I am in danger right now. Is Spectra the right place?
No. Contact people who can act now: the police, someone you trust, or an organisation that works on your situation.
Spectra is information, not an emergency service, and following it guarantees nothing.

## games
### What is Real or scam?
[Real or scam](/real-or-scam) is a game of four messages a round, where you say whether each one is real or a scam and then see which trick each scam uses.
Nothing is timed.

### What does "Which tricks work on you" find out?
[Which tricks work on you](/quiz) shows which tricks could get past you, and the step for each.
You get seven everyday situations and pick what you would most likely do, not what you think is the right answer.

### Do my answers change my list?
Your answers in the game and the quiz change only the order of your list, never which steps are in it.
A scam that gets past you, or an answer showing a trick could work on you, moves the step for that trick earlier. A step is never moved later, and your answers stay in your browser.

## trust
### Is Spectra professional, legal or security advice?
No. Spectra is educational information, not professional advice, and following it guarantees nothing.
Each step says what is generally worth doing and links its source, so you can read the reasoning and decide for yourself. [Read the full disclaimer](/about#disclaimer).

### Can I see how Spectra works, down to the code?
Yes. Spectra's code is open source under AGPL-3.0, and all of the site's text is under CC BY-SA 4.0, both on [GitHub](https://github.com/KashishOO7/spectra).
Spectra runs entirely in your browser. The order of the steps comes from code anyone can read, and [Method and sources](/methodology) explains it in plain words.

### Does Spectra work in my country?
Yes. Spectra is written for readers in any country.
Where something works differently from place to place, the step says so and links the source.

### Is Spectra available in other languages?
No. Spectra is in English only.

### How do I report a mistake?
Open an issue on [Spectra's GitHub page](https://github.com/KashishOO7/spectra/issues), and say which page and which sentence is wrong.
