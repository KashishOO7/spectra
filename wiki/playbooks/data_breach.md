

## title
My details were in a data breach

## subtitle
A service you use says someone got into the details it holds about you

## do-not
Do not trust a breach notification email on its own. Check the notice on the company's official website, opened yourself, not through a link in the email.

## technical
- Change your password on the breached service now, even if the company says passwords were not exposed.
- Check haveibeenpwned.com with your email address to see what data is associated with this breach and what else may be exposed.
- If you use the same password anywhere else: change it everywhere, because logins leaked from one site are tried on others.
- Turn on two-factor authentication on the affected service if it is off.
- If the breach included payment card data: contact your bank to flag the card for suspicious activity or request a replacement.
- If the breach included your national ID number or passport: check your credit report, where your country has them, for accounts you did not open. Where the law allows it, also put a credit freeze on your file, which blocks new credit in your name.

## plain
- Change your password on that website now, even if they say passwords were not affected.
- Go to haveibeenpwned.com and type in your email address to see what else may have been exposed.
- Did you use the same password on other sites? Change it everywhere it was used.
- Turn on two-factor authentication on the affected account if it is off.
- If your payment card was included: call your bank and ask them to flag it or send you a new card.
- If your ID card or passport details were included: check your credit report, where your country has them, for loans or cards you did not open. In some countries you can also put a credit freeze on your file, so no one can open new credit in your name.
