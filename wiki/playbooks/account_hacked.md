

## title
Someone got into my account

## subtitle
You can't sign in, or you see things in it you did not do

## do-not
Do not do the recovery on the device you think was broken into. Use your phone or a different computer.

## technical
- From a different device, go to the service's own sign-in page and reset the password with its forgot password link.
- Sign out every other session: look for "Active sessions", "Devices" or "Security" in the account's settings.
- Change the password on every other account that uses the same password, or a similar one.
- Turn on two-factor authentication if it was off, so a password alone is no longer enough to get in.
- Check the account's forwarding rules and connected third-party apps, and remove any you did not set up, because a password change does not remove them.
- If it is your email account, also check its filters and its recovery email and phone number.
- If your phone number can reset the account, ask your mobile provider whether they can lock the number against being moved to another SIM.

## plain
- Use a different device, your phone or a friend's computer, not the one you think was broken into.
- Go to the website, press "Forgot password" and reset it to a new password you have not used before.
- Once back in, look for "Active sessions" or "Devices" in Settings and sign out of everything.
- If you used the same password anywhere else, change it on those sites too.
- Turn on two-factor authentication, so a password alone is no longer enough to get in.
- If it was your email: check Settings for any forwarding rules or auto-replies you did not set up.
- Look for "Connected apps", "Third-party access" or "Apps with account access" in Settings and remove anything you do not recognise. A password change does not lock these out.
- If your phone number can reset the account, ask your mobile provider whether they can lock the number against being moved to another SIM.
