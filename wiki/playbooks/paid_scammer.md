
## title
I paid a scammer

## subtitle
You paid someone who tricked you, by card, bank transfer, payment app, gift card, cryptocurrency or cash

## do-not
Stop replying to the scammer and send no more money, whatever they promise next. If someone offers to get your money back for a fee, do not pay. That offer is a second scam.

## technical
- Call your bank or card company now. For a card payment, use the number on the back of the card or your banking app. For a payment app or a money transfer company, report the payment in the app or on the company's website.
- Say the payment was fraud and ask for it to be reversed, even if the money seems gone, because the sooner the bank or company knows, the better the chance of getting it back.
- If the scammer has your card number, ask the bank or card company to cancel the card and send a new one, then update any regular payments with the new number. If they have your online banking login, ask your bank to block online banking and help you set a new password.
- Keep screenshots of your messages with the scammer, the transaction number and the account or wallet address the money went to, because the company you paid through and the police may ask for them.
- For a gift card, call the company that issued it, report that a scammer has the card's number and PIN, and ask for a refund. Keep the card and its receipt.
- Cash sent by post or courier can sometimes be stopped before it is delivered, so call the postal service or courier straight away.
- For cryptocurrency, report the transfer to the exchange you sent it from, or the company that runs the cryptocurrency ATM you used, and ask whether they can stop it, though cryptocurrency is hard to get back.
- Report the scam to the police or to the official fraud reporting service where you live.
- If the scammer had you install a remote access app, which lets them control your phone or computer, uninstall it. Then, from another phone or computer, change the passwords of your important accounts, starting with your email.
- If you gave the scammer a password, change it on every account where you used it, and turn on two-factor authentication on those accounts.

## plain
- Call your bank or card company now. For a card payment, use the number on the back of the card. If you paid through a payment app or a money transfer service, report the payment inside it.
- Tell whoever you contact that you were scammed, and ask them to reverse the payment, even if the money seems gone. The sooner they know, the better your chance of getting it back.
- If you gave the scammer your card number or your online banking login, ask your bank to block the card or lock your online banking when you call.
- Keep the messages, screenshots and the transaction number, because the bank and the police will ask for them.
- With a gift card, call the company named on the card, ask for a refund, and keep the card, because they may ask for it.
- Cash sent by post or courier can sometimes be stopped before it arrives, so call the postal service or courier you used straight away.
- Cryptocurrency is hard to get back, but tell the exchange you used, or the company named on the cryptocurrency ATM, that you were scammed, and ask whether they can stop the payment or get it back.
- Report the scam to the police or to the official fraud reporting service where you live.
- If the scammer had you install an app to control your phone or computer, remove it, and change the passwords of your important accounts, starting with your email and your bank, from another phone or computer.
- If you gave the scammer a password, change it on every account where you used it, and turn on two-factor authentication on those accounts.
