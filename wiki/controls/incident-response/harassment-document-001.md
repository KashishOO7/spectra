## Why
Harassment online is common, and it does not only come from someone you know. It can come from a stranger, or from someone at school or work. Whatever you decide to do about it later, reporting it to a service, telling your work or going to the police, starts with a record of what happened.
## What
Start keeping a record now, before any of it is deleted. Saving the messages is the last thing you want to do when you want it to stop, but asking a service to act, telling your work and going to the police all need it.
## How
### all
Save it before it goes
Take screenshots, and save the link to each post or profile as well. Posts get deleted, by the person or by the service, and a screenshot with no link behind it is weaker evidence later.
Keep the messages themselves rather than forwarding them on. Forwarding can strip information that is attached to the original.

Save your own replies too
If you answered angrily, save that too. A record with your own replies removed can be used against you later, and you do not have to have behaved perfectly to be entitled to act.

Let somebody else do it if you can
Going through it repeatedly is its own harm. If there is someone you trust, ask them to collect it for you. Some services, Gmail among them, let you give another person access to your account for this without sharing your password.

Why the record is worth the effort
A record shows the pattern. One message can be dismissed, but forty from the same person are hard to ignore.
A record also shows that a new account belongs to the same person, when someone who was blocked comes back under a different name.

Then block, and tell the people close to you
Block the account. Expect it not to be the end of it, and keep saving what arrives.
Tell the people around you what is happening. If it has reached your work, tell them before they hear it from somewhere else.

If you feel unsafe
Take it seriously and trust that feeling rather than talking yourself out of it. Getting somewhere you feel safe comes before any of the above.
If you are in danger now, call your local emergency number. The record is what you will need if you decide to involve the police.
## Law
### global
What counts as harassment, and what a service or a court will act on, differs from country to country. This is general information, not legal advice, and someone qualified where you live can tell you your rights.
