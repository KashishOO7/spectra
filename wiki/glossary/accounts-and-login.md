
## two-factor authentication
also: 2FA, two-step login, two-step verification, second factor

A way of signing in that asks for a second proof after your password, usually a code or an approval on your phone.

## credentials
also: credential

The username and password you use to sign in to an account.

## password manager

An app that creates a different password for every account and saves them for you.

## authenticator app
also: authenticator

An app on your phone that shows a login code to type in after your password. The code keeps changing, and the app works without an internet connection or a phone signal.

## backup codes
also: backup code, recovery codes

A set of codes that many websites let you save once two-factor authentication is on. Each code works once, in place of the code from your phone, if you lose the phone or cannot get codes on it.

## passphrase

A password made of several words chosen at random. Its length makes it hard to guess, and real words are easier to remember than random letters and symbols.

## credential stuffing
also: credential-stuffing

Attackers take usernames and passwords stolen from one site and use a program to try them on many other sites, looking for people who reused the same password.

## SIM swap
also: SIM-swapping, SIM swapping

Someone tricks your mobile provider into giving them your phone number, so your calls and texts, including login codes, go to their phone instead of yours.

## passkey
also: passkeys

A way of signing in to a site or app without a password. Your device or password manager stores a passkey for each account, and you approve each sign-in with your fingerprint, face or screen lock.

## sign-in prompt
also: sign-in prompts

A message the company behind an account sends to a phone where you are already signed in, asking you to approve or refuse a sign-in.

## account takeover

Someone else gets into your account and uses it as you. They may also change the password or recovery details so that you cannot get back in.

## email alias
also: email aliases

An extra email address whose mail arrives in your main inbox, so you can give it to a website instead of your main address and turn it off on its own.

## trusted contact
also: trusted contacts

A person a customer names in advance, whom a bank or investment firm may call if it cannot reach the customer or thinks the account is being misused. They cannot move the money.

## power of attorney

A legal arrangement, in many countries, that lets someone manage another person's money or decisions on their behalf.
