# Three questions before you act on a message
Spelling mistakes used to be a common sign of a scam message. Scammers now use AI tools that write with correct spelling, so these three questions look at the situation you are in rather than at how the message is written.

## Rows

### Did I start this?
Initiation
Ask whether you contacted them first, or they contacted you. A message that arrived without you starting anything deserves more suspicion than a reply to something you sent.

### Is it rushing me, scaring me, or offering me something?
Urgency · Fear · Reward · Authority
Scammers add pressure so that you act before you check. A real problem can wait the few minutes it takes to check.

### Is it asking me to do something?
Action
The harm usually comes when you act: clicking a link, opening a file, typing a password or sending money. A message that asks nothing may still be a first step, so check again when a later one asks for something.

## Notes
- If a message arrived without you starting anything, puts pressure on you and asks you to act, treat it as a scam until you have checked.
- To check, contact the organisation or person another way. Type the website address yourself, or call a number you already had, such as the one on your bank card. Never use a number or link from the message.
